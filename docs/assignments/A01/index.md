# Task B: Product Analysis
a) Primary Function: The mechanical pencil's main function is to take a small axial force from my hand at the push-button and turn it into a controlled linear movement of graphite. It does this through a spring-loaded clutch that grips and releases the lead. In other words, the pencil's job is to advance the graphite in a predictable amount each time I press the button.

b) Governing Model: i) The behavior of the clutch is governed by friction between the jaws and graphite. The basic model is: F_f = uN   
F_f = friction force holding the lead
u = coefficient of friction between the clutch jaws and the graphite
N = normal force created when the jaws are pushed into the tapered section of the barrel
This friction is what keeps the lead from sliding out while I'm writing.
ii) I'm assuming the clutch jaws act like rigid bodies and that the friction coefficient between the jaws and the graphite stays basically constant while the pencil is being used. Without that assumption, the model wouldn't describe the gripping behavior accurately.

The barrel is the long cylindrical tube that holds all the internal parts. Its geometry forces the clutch and lead to move only along one axis, which prevents wobbling and keeps the pencil stable while writing. The internal diameter is sized so the clutch stays centered and the lead stays straight.

Clutch Mechanism: <img width="500" height="502" alt="802ac12e823b3e1b5443959ed182d525" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fbb4d8dc-bac9-49ed-b04c-efb58acc79f1" />  The clutch has small jaws with tapered outer surfaces. When the push-button compresses the spring, the jaws slide forward into a conical section of the barrel. That taper increases the normal force on the graphite, which increases friction. The geometry basically turns axial motion into radial gripping force, allowing the pencil to hold or release the lead depending on the applied force.

The spring is a helical coil that stores energy when compressed and resets the mechanism when released. Its coil diameter and wire thickness determine how much force is needed to advance the lead.

d) Patent Research: A common mechanical pencil design is covered under US2920877A, titled "propelling Pencil". The inventor/author is Charles R. Keeran. 
i) Two other devices that accomplish the same primary function would be a standard wooden pencil, or a drafting lead holder. They both deliver graphite to a writing surface but use different mechanical systems.
ii) Design Decision: One clear design decision in the patent is the use of a tapered clutch instead of a straight cylindrical one. The taper increases normal force automatically as the jaws move forward, which increases friction without adding extra parts. I think the engineer chose this because it gives reliable gripping with not much complexity.
